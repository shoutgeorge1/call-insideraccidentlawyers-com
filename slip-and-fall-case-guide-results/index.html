Educational summary

Issues an attorney would examine

Based on the information you provided, here are the main issues an attorney would look at. Additional facts may be important — an attorney must review the complete facts before any conclusion.

What an attorney would examine

The location and who controlled it, the dangerous condition, whether the owner knew or should have known, what warnings or repairs were in place, the seriousness of the injury, the medical treatment, and the evidence available.

Time-sensitive

Immediate evidence preservation

Some evidence — especially surveillance video — can disappear quickly. The steps below are worth doing as soon as possible.

Preservation checklist

  • Save the clothing and footwear from the incident.
  • Back up every photo and video you have of the hazard, scene, and injuries (do not edit originals).
  • Write down the exact time and location.
  • Write down the names of employees, managers, and witnesses you spoke with.
  • Request an incident report and keep a copy.
  • Save receipts or any proof you were present.
  • Preserve all medical documents (ER, urgent care, imaging, follow-up).
  • Document missed work and lost wages.
  • Ask in writing that surveillance footage be preserved.
  • Avoid posting detailed statements about the incident publicly.
  • Speak with an attorney before signing any release or giving a recorded statement to insurance.
