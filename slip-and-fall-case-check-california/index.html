What we look at first

A quick review usually starts with three practical questions.

Where it happened

Stores, rental common areas, hotels, parking lots, sidewalks, workplaces and homes can involve different insurance and responsible parties.

How serious the injury was

Documented injuries, ER care, fractures, surgery, head injuries, or ongoing treatment generally make review more urgent.

What evidence exists

Incident reports, photos, video, witnesses, employee involvement, and surveillance can shape what happens next.

Why location matters

Commercial properties, rental common areas, parking areas, public property, workplaces, and homes may involve different responsible parties. The question is not just who owns the property, but who controlled, maintained, cleaned, repaired, warned about, or created the condition.

Surveillance and incident evidence can disappear. A prompt review may help preserve it.

Quick questions

Does a fall automatically mean there is a case?

No. The review usually looks at the location, what caused the fall, who controlled the area, injury severity, medical treatment, and available evidence.

What if there was a warning sign?

A sign can matter, but it does not automatically answer the whole question. Visibility, placement, whether it blocked the danger, and whether you had a practical reason to pass through the area can all matter.

What if I fell on public property or at work?

Public property may involve special claim procedures and shorter deadlines. Workplace injuries may involve workers' compensation, a third-party property claim, or both. Prompt review can be important.
