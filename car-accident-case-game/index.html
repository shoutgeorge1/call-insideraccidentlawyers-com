What to know after a car accident

A short, honest overview — not legal advice. The Case Check above is the fastest way to get your situation looked at.

When a car accident may be worth reviewing

Cases we often hear about include:

  • You were hurt and saw a doctor, ER, or chiropractor.
  • Another driver was clearly or mostly at fault.
  • The crash caused medical bills, missed work, or ongoing pain.
  • An insurance company is pressuring you to sign or settle quickly.

What information helps your case

  • Police or incident report number (if you have one).
  • Photos of the scene, vehicles, and any visible injuries.
  • Names and contact info of witnesses.
  • Notes about who you spoke to at each insurance company.
  • Medical records, ER paperwork, and bills as they come in.

Why speaking with insurance first can be risky

Insurance adjusters often call shortly after an accident. They may ask for a recorded statement or float a quick offer. You generally don’t have to give a statement to the other driver’s insurance, and an early offer may not reflect the full picture of your medical costs, missed work, or recovery.

Common questions

Short answers to what people usually ask before reaching out.

How much does the Case Check cost?
The Case Check and follow-up review are free and confidential. There are no fees to talk with our team. If we represent you, our fee comes from any recovery — not out of pocket.
How long do I have to take action?
California generally allows two years from the date of a personal injury to file a claim, but some situations are shorter. The sooner you talk with someone, the more options you typically have.
What if I’m not sure I have a case?
That’s normal. The Case Check is designed to help you and us understand whether your situation may be worth a closer look. Either way, you’ll get a straight answer.
Do I have to talk to the other driver’s insurance?
Generally, no. You usually aren’t required to give a recorded statement to the other driver’s insurance company, and it’s often best to speak with someone on your side first.
