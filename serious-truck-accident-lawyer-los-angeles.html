Multiple Companies May Be Responsible.

Truck accident cases are often far more layered than a typical car crash. More defendants can mean more records, more insurance layers, and more pressure—exactly why early investigation matters.

  • Trucking company
  • Driver employer
  • Cargo loader
  • Maintenance vendor
  • Vehicle manufacturer

Truck Accident Claims Are Complex and High Stakes.

One collision can implicate drivers, motor carriers, brokers, maintenance vendors, and shippers. We know where to look—and what to preserve.

  • Multiple liable companies — not just “the driver.”
  • Federal trucking regulations (hours, maintenance, qualification).
  • Driver fatigue patterns and log inconsistencies.
  • Black box and telematics data before it’s overwritten or “lost.”
  • Large insurance defense teams trained to minimize your claim.

Trucking Companies Begin Their Defense Immediately.

Spoliation letters, rapid records requests, and expert involvement are not “extras” in trucking cases—they are baseline survival tactics for a serious claim.

  • Accident reconstruction experts when liability is disputed or complex.
  • Driver log, dispatch, and compliance file review.
  • Surveillance and dashcam retrieval where available.
  • Witness identification and early statements while memories are fresh.

Serious Truck Injury Claims Can Involve Significant Compensation.

When a commercial vehicle changes your body, your career, or your family’s future, catastrophic damages can include lifetime medical care, permanent disability, and loss of earning capacity—with wrongful death exposure when a family loses a breadwinner. Substantial recovery depends on investigation and trial leverage, not adjuster sympathy.

Commercial trucking policies often exceed $1M in available coverage—and multiple layers may apply.

  • Lifetime medical care, rehab, and attendant needs
  • Spinal injuries, surgical intervention, and long inpatient recovery
  • Traumatic brain injury and cognitive impairment
  • Permanent disability and documented loss of earning capacity
  • Wrongful death exposure: lost support, services, and consortium
