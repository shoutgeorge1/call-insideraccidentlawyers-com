When a property injury may be worth reviewing

Stronger fact patterns

  • Serious injury — broken bone, head injury, ER care
  • Commercial property — store, restaurant, complex
  • Treatment soon after the incident
  • Clear hazard the property owner could have addressed

What evidence helps

  • Incident report from the store, complex, or owner
  • Photos of the hazard and injury
  • Names of any witnesses
  • Security camera footage
  • Medical records from after the injury

Why timing matters

California has time limits on property injury claims, and claims against public entities can have much shorter deadlines. Calling sooner gives an attorney more time to gather evidence and preserve your rights.

Common questions

Does the Evidence Check decide my case?

No. It's a quick way to organize what happened so an attorney can review it. Every case is unique and depends on the facts.

What does a free review cost?

Nothing. The consultation is free and there is no fee unless we win your case.

Do I need to have photos or an incident report?

No. Cases can still be reviewed without them — those just make the picture clearer faster.

I was hurt on government property — is it too late?

Claims involving public property can have shorter deadlines, so timing may matter. The fastest way to know is a free call.

I was hurt at work — can you help?

Work injuries may involve a different process than a property injury claim. If a non-employer (like a property owner or contractor) was also involved, give us a call so we can take a closer look.

How long does it take?

The Evidence Check takes about 60 seconds. A short call with our team usually takes 5–10 minutes.
