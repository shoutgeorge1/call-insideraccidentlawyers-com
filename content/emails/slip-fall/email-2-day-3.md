Hi {{ first_name }},

One of the first things an attorney looks at is **where** the fall
happened — because the location often decides who may be responsible and
what kind of records exist.

A short tour:

**Stores, restaurants, hotels, big-box retailers.**
Commercial properties usually involve a business operator, a property
owner, and one or more insurance policies. Many have inspection logs,
cleaning schedules, incident-report systems, and surveillance.

**Apartments and rental property.**
Common areas — stairs, walkways, hallways, parking, laundry rooms — are
usually under landlord or property-management control. Maintenance
requests and prior-complaint records can be important.

**Commercial parking lots.**
Can involve the business, a separate property owner, a management
company, or a maintenance vendor. Identifying who controlled the area is
often the first step.

**Public sidewalks or government property.**
May involve a city, county, school district, transit agency, or another
public entity. Special claim procedures and shorter deadlines may apply,
so prompt review can be important.

**Workplaces.**
Workers' compensation, a third-party property claim, or both may need to
be considered depending on who controlled or created the dangerous
condition.

**Your own home.**
A traditional third-party property claim is less likely. A contractor,
product, landlord, or maintenance company could still matter depending
on the facts.
